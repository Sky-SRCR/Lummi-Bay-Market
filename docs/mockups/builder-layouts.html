Three ways to lay out the Builder

The properties panel floats over the toolbars because its position is a guess at how tall they are. Every option below docks it into the layout instead — and each one spends the space it frees differently. Switch between the Admin and Basic user view to compare what each role actually sees. B was chosen — the settled design is the last panel.

What is happening today

A panel positioned 31 pixels above where the toolbars end

The properties panel is position: fixed; right: 16px; top: 100px. That 100px is a fixed guess at the height of a stack that is not a fixed height.

The chrome above the canvas is a flex column, and it grows: the nav is 34–46px, the control bar is about 44px and wraps to a second row on a narrow window, and the align bar adds roughly 41px more. The align bar and the properties panel are revealed by the same event — selecting a block calls showInspector(), which calls updateAlignBar() on its third line. So the two are never on screen separately, and the panel starts inside the bar every single time.

The reading you got from it
A panel that overlaps the furniture behind it, casts a drop shadow and has a title bar looks like a window somebody can pick up and move. Nothing in the Builder can drag it. The overlap is not just untidy — it is the page offering a control that does not exist.

Option A

Docked rail — the same Builder, with the panel let into the wall

Smallest change

Everything above the canvas stays exactly where it is. The editor area becomes a row of two: canvas on the left, a 232–290px properties rail on the right that is always there.

Because the rail is a sibling of the canvas rather than a layer over it, no overlap is arithmetically possible — the toolbars can wrap to three rows and the rail just gets shorter. It also stops the panel appearing and vanishing under the pointer: when nothing is selected the rail says so, which is a better answer than an empty screen edge.

Option A — the rail is part of the layout, so the bars above can grow freely.

What changes

  • The editor area becomes a flex row; the panel moves inside it.
  • position: fixed and top: 100px both go away.
  • The rail keeps a resting state: “Select a block to edit it.”
  • The Basic user’s section-targeting tip lives in the rail instead of an orange banner that only shows sometimes.

What it costs

  • The canvas loses about 240px of width permanently.
  • Five stacked bars are still five stacked bars — on a 1366×768 laptop the canvas gets roughly 560px of height.
  • Nothing else gets better; it fixes the overlap and stops there.

Right for you if

You like the Builder and only want the panel to stop lying about being draggable. This is the one change that can ship on its own without anybody having to relearn where a button is.

Option B

Workbench — tools left, canvas centre, properties right

Moderate change

The add-block buttons leave the top bar and become a vertical palette on the left, grouped by what they are: Layout, Text, Media. The top bar shrinks to one row — who you are, which sign, Undo, Publish.

This is the layout that most helps the Basic user. Today a basic account sees a wide bar holding four orange buttons and a lot of nothing, because six of the ten controls are admin-only and simply absent. In a vertical palette, four items is a short list rather than a half-empty shelf — and the palette is where the “pick a section first” instruction belongs, next to the buttons it is about.

The align bar goes too. Its buttons become an Arrange group in the properties rail, which is where the rest of the selected block’s controls already are, and means one less bar that appears and disappears as you click around.

Option B — switch to Basic user: the palette shortens honestly instead of a bar emptying out.

What changes

  • Add-block buttons become a grouped left palette.
  • The top bar drops to a single 46px row.
  • Align tools move into the rail as Arrange; the align bar is retired.
  • Background controls become a palette item that opens in the rail.

What it costs

  • Two columns of chrome — roughly 390px of width gone before the canvas starts.
  • Every button a person already knows the position of has moved.
  • The help.php walkthrough and its screenshots describe the old bar.

Right for you if

The Builder is going to keep gaining block types. A vertical palette absorbs a seventh and eighth block type; a horizontal bar wraps to a second row and shoves the canvas down.

Option C

Canvas first — one bar, a drawer that pushes, and a status line

Largest change

The sign is the point, so the sign gets the room. One top bar with an + Add menu; zoom, selection and publish state move to a thin status line at the bottom; the properties panel becomes a drawer that pushes the canvas aside and can be closed when you want the whole board.

The five notice bars — display turned off, somebody else is editing, the lock has lapsed, your access was removed — collapse into one chip in the top bar that expands to the full sentence. They keep their wording and their colour; what they stop doing is each taking a permanent 35px slice off the top of a 768px screen.

One thing to decide first
The access-removed and read-only notices are the ones a person must not miss. A chip is not the right home for those two: they would stay as full bars, and only the informational notices collapse. Worth agreeing before this gets built.

Option C — the drawer pushes rather than floats, and the grip is a real handle for closing it.

What changes

  • All add-block controls move into one + Add menu.
  • Zoom, selection, targeted section, publish state and lock age go to a status line.
  • The properties drawer opens and closes, and pushes the canvas rather than covering it.
  • Informational notices collapse to a chip; the two that must be read stay as bars.

What it costs

  • Adding a block is two clicks instead of one.
  • A menu needs open/close, keyboard and click-outside behaviour that no bar needed.
  • The biggest rewrite of the three, and the biggest relearn.

Right for you if

Signs are being built on a laptop and the canvas keeps getting zoomed to 60% to fit. This buys back around 120px of height — the difference between a 1920×1080 board at 79% and at 100%.

The recommendation, and what was chosen

The recommendation on this page was A now, B next — A being the smallest thing that makes the overlap impossible, B being the one that fixes the Basic user’s half-empty toolbar. B was chosen, with the nav reworked and a canvas footer added. The settled design is below; A and C stay on this page as the record of what it was chosen against.

Settled · 13 August 2026

Option B as specified

B’s three columns, a rebuilt nav, and a canvas footer that B as pitched didn’t have. Two slots are filled by later work — the Brand control and the Workspace Theme item — and are drawn here so the layout can be judged whole.

Four things changed from the pitch above. The nav drops the store logo and the role chip, and its links move behind a gear. Sign Out stays visible — it was the one link that shouldn’t need a click to find. The canvas footer is new: it carries the zoom controls, which B as pitched had simply lost, and the last-published line, which belongs on screen because the question it answers — is what I’m looking at live? — has to answer at a glance. And the Brand control sits with the sign’s other facts, while the Workspace Theme sits in the gear with the person’s: two pickers, opposite ends of the bar, describing different nouns.

  • Canvas footer — zoom left, last-published right.
